Connected TV (CTV) CPM Guide: Hulu, Roku, YouTube TV in 2026

CTV ad spending hit $33.4 billion in 2026. Here are the CPM benchmarks for every major platform, the AVOD vs SVOD economics, and how CTV compares to linear TV and digital display.

Connected TV is the fastest-growing segment of the US ad market. In 2026, US CTV ad spending reached $33.4 billion, up from $28.8 billion in 2025 and just $7.1 billion in 2020. That is a 5-year CAGR of 36%. But CTV is not one market — it is a fragmented landscape of 15+ platforms with CPMs ranging from $10 (Pluto TV) to $42 (Disney+). This guide maps every major platform, explains why the spread is so wide, and shows you how to buy efficiently.

What Is Connected TV?

Connected TV (CTV) refers to any television connected to the internet that can stream video content. This includes:

  • Smart TVs with built-in apps (Samsung Tizen, LG webOS, Vizio SmartCast)
  • Streaming devices plugged into a TV (Roku, Amazon Fire TV, Apple TV, Google Chromecast)
  • Gaming consoles used for streaming (PlayStation, Xbox)
  • Linear streaming services that replace cable (YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV, Sling TV, FuboTV)

The key distinction from linear TV: CTV delivers ads via internet streaming, which means every impression can be targeted, measured, and bought programmatically. There is no Nielsen panel estimation — you get actual impression-level data. This precision is why CTV CPM is higher than linear TV despite smaller audiences: advertisers pay a premium for targetability.

Market size: In 2026, 248 million US adults watch CTV at least monthly — 73% of the adult population. Linear TV reaches 256 million, but CTV is growing while linear declines. By 2028, CTV ad spend is projected to overtake linear TV ad spend entirely. Sources: eMarketer Connected TV ad spending forecast, Statista Connected TV market data.

AVOD vs SVOD vs TVOD: Why CPM Differs

Understanding the three monetization models is essential to understanding CTV CPM. Each model creates a different advertising inventory supply, which drives different CPM levels.

Sources: Monetization model definitions and CPM ranges from IAB Connected TV Playbook 2026 and Innovid CTV Benchmark Report. The hybrid model (ad-supported tier of a subscription service) has become dominant since 2023, when Netflix and Disney+ both launched ad tiers. AVOD inventory grew 45% YoY in 2026 as FAST channels proliferated.

The economic logic:

  • AVOD platforms need to fill more ad slots (more content, more ad breaks) so they sell inventory at lower CPM. Volume compensates for price. Pluto TV runs 4–6 minutes of ads per hour; Disney+ runs 3–4 minutes.
  • Hybrid platforms have less ad inventory (subscribers see fewer ads) but higher-quality audiences (paying households have higher income). Less supply + higher demand = higher CPM.
  • Pure SVOD (Netflix Premium, Apple TV+) has no ad inventory. The "ad tier" versions of these services are technically hybrid.

The takeaway: CTV sits in the sweet spot between linear TV and digital display. It has the big-screen impact and completion rates of linear TV, with the targeting and measurement of digital. Its CPM is lower than primetime linear TV ($10–$42 vs $25–$55) because there is more inventory and less upfront commitment. Its CPM is higher than display because a 30-second ad on a 55-inch screen during premium content is worth more than a banner on a website.

How to Buy CTV Ads

There are three ways to buy CTV inventory, each with different minimums, CPMs, and control levels:

1. Direct Buy (Publisher IO)

You buy directly from the platform (Hulu, Roku, Disney+). Minimum spend is typically $25,000–$50,000 per quarter. You get guaranteed inventory, premium placements, and custom creative options. CPM is at the higher end of the range because you are buying guaranteed primetime inventory. Best for: large brands with big budgets who need guaranteed reach on specific content.

2. Programmatic / DSP (Open Marketplace)

You buy through a demand-side platform (The Trade Desk, DV360, Amazon DSP) connected to CTV supply via SSPs. Minimum spend can be as low as $500. You get granular targeting, frequency capping, and cross-platform reach in one buy. CPM is typically 15–30% lower than direct because inventory is non-guaranteed (remnant or open auction). Best for: performance-oriented buyers, SMB budgets, and campaigns needing cross-platform reach.

3. Private Marketplace (PMP / Programmatic Guaranteed)

A hybrid: you get access to a publisher's premium inventory via a deal ID, but transact programmatically. Minimum spend is $5,000–$10,000. You get guaranteed inventory (like direct) with programmatic targeting (like open). CPM sits between direct and open. Best for: mid-size brands who want premium inventory with targeting control.

Why CTV CPM Is Rising

CTV CPM has increased ~12% per year since 2023. Three forces are driving this:

  1. Demand is growing faster than supply. Ad spend grew from $21B (2024) to $33.4B (2026), a 59% increase. Ad-supported inventory grew ~45% in the same period. When demand outpaces supply, CPM rises. Source: eMarketer CTV ad spending forecast.
  2. Premium content migration. Sports and live events are moving to CTV (NFL on Amazon Prime, NBA on NBC/Peacock starting 2025, MLB on Apple TV+). Premium live content commands the highest CPM because it has the largest concentrated audience. Source: Statista sports streaming data.
  3. Measurement is improving. As CTV measurement matures (TAM 2.0, Video Nexus, Nielsen ONE), advertisers gain confidence that impressions are real and measurable. This confidence increases willingness to bid higher. Source: IAB CTV measurement initiatives.

The trend is expected to continue through 2028, with CTV CPM growing 8–10% annually as linear TV budgets shift to CTV and the supply of premium ad inventory remains constrained.

FAQ

What is the average CPM for Connected TV?

The US average across all CTV platforms is approximately $25–$30 in 2026. This ranges from $10 (Pluto TV, FAST channels) to $42 (Disney+ ad-supported tier). Source: eMarketer CTV ad spending forecast.

Is CTV CPM higher than linear TV?

Not necessarily. Linear TV primetime CPM averages $25–$55; CTV averages $10–$42. CTV is often cheaper than primetime linear TV, with better targeting. However, CTV is more expensive than digital display ($0.50–$8). Source: Statista.

What is the cheapest CTV platform for advertising?

FAST (free ad-supported streaming TV) platforms have the lowest CPM: Pluto TV at ~$10 and Tubi at ~$15. These are AVOD-only platforms with high inventory volume. Source: IAB Connected TV Playbook.

What is the minimum spend for CTV advertising?

Through programmatic buying (The Trade Desk, DV360), you can start with $500. Direct buys from publishers typically require $25,000–$50,000 per quarter. Private marketplaces sit in between at $5,000–$10,000. Source: IAB CTV Programmatic Guide.

How much of CTV ad spend is programmatic?

70% of US CTV ad spend is transacted programmatically in 2026, up from 55% in 2024. By 2028, eMarketer projects 80% will be programmatic. Source: eMarketer programmatic CTV forecast.

Should I use CTV or YouTube for video ads?

They serve different goals. YouTube CPM averages $5–$10 (cheaper, broader reach, more skippable). CTV CPM averages $25–$30 (more expensive, premium content, higher completion rates, big-screen viewing). For brand campaigns, CTV; for performance and reach, YouTube.
