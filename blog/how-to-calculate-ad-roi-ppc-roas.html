How to Calculate Ad ROI & PPC ROAS (With Formulas)

Master the two most important profitability metrics in digital advertising with formulas, benchmarks, and optimization strategies.

Spending money on advertising is only worthwhile if it generates a positive return. ROI (Return on Investment) and ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) are the two metrics that tell you whether your advertising is profitable. Understanding how to calculate and interpret them is essential for every marketer, business owner, and media buyer.

What Is PPC ROI?

PPC ROI (Pay-Per-Click Return on Investment) measures the total profitability of your advertising campaign as a percentage. It accounts for all costs (ad spend, tools, labor) and all revenue generated. ROI tells you whether your advertising made money or lost money overall.

A positive ROI means your campaign generated more revenue than it cost. A negative ROI means you spent more than you earned. The average PPC ROI across all industries is approximately 200%, meaning businesses earn $2 for every $1 spent on advertising.

What Is PPC ROAS?

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) measures the revenue generated for every dollar spent on advertising. Unlike ROI, ROAS focuses specifically on ad spend (not total costs) and is expressed as a ratio rather than a percentage.

A ROAS of 4:1 means you earn $4 in revenue for every $1 spent on ads. The average ROAS across all industries is approximately 2:1 ($2 earned per $1 spent), though this varies dramatically by industry.

The ROI Formula

ROI = ((Revenue - Total Cost) / Total Cost) × 100

Example

You spend $5,000 on a Google Ads campaign (including ad spend, agency fees, and tool costs). The campaign generates $15,000 in revenue.

ROI = (($15,000 - $5,000) / $5,000) × 100 = 200%

Your ROI is 200%, meaning you earned $2 in profit for every $1 invested.

The ROAS Formula

ROAS = Revenue / Ad Spend

Example

You spend $5,000 on Google Ads. The campaign generates $15,000 in revenue.

ROAS = $15,000 / $5,000 = 3:1 (or 3x)

Your ROAS is 3:1, meaning you earned $3 for every $1 spent on ads.

ROI vs ROAS: ROI accounts for all costs (including labor, tools, overhead) and is expressed as a percentage. ROAS only considers ad spend and is expressed as a ratio. A campaign can have a positive ROAS but a negative ROI if non-ad costs are high enough.

How to Track ROI Across Channels

Accurate ROI calculation requires proper tracking. Here is how to track ROI across major channels:

  • Google Ads: Use conversion tracking pixels, Google Analytics 4 integration, and UTM parameters to attribute revenue to specific campaigns.
  • Facebook/Instagram: Use the Facebook Pixel, Conversions API, and catalog sales tracking. iOS 14.5+ has limited tracking, so use server-side tracking where possible.
  • Email marketing: Track revenue from email campaigns using platform analytics (Klaviyo, Mailchimp) and attribute based on click-through.
  • Organic search: Use Google Search Console and GA4 to track organic traffic revenue. Compare against the cost of SEO (content, technical optimization).
  • Content marketing: Track revenue from blog posts, landing pages, and lead magnets using attribution modeling.

Google Ads Average ROAS by Industry

Google Ads specific ROAS benchmarks for 2026:

  • Search ads average ROAS: 3.5:1 across all industries
  • Display ads average ROAS: 1.5:1 (lower due to brand awareness focus)
  • Shopping ads average ROAS: 8:1 (highest due to direct purchase intent)
  • Video ads average ROAS: 2:1 (brand awareness and consideration)
  • Performance Max average ROAS: 4:1 (AI-optimized across all channels)

Tips to Improve PPC ROI

  1. Improve Quality Score: Higher Quality Scores in Google Ads reduce your CPC, directly improving ROI. Focus on ad relevance, landing page experience, and expected CTR.
  2. Target high-intent keywords: Focus on keywords with clear purchase intent ("buy," "best," "near me") rather than informational queries.
  3. Optimize landing pages: A well-optimized landing page can double your conversion rate, directly doubling your ROI.
  4. Use negative keywords: Filter out irrelevant traffic that wastes budget without converting.
  5. Implement remarketing: Retargeting campaigns typically have 3-5x higher ROAS than cold traffic campaigns.
  6. Test ad copy: A/B test headlines, descriptions, and CTAs. Small improvements in CTR compound into significant ROI gains.
  7. Optimize for mobile: Over 60% of search ads are clicked on mobile. Ensure your landing pages are mobile-optimized.
  8. Track all costs: Include agency fees, tool costs, and labor in your ROI calculation to get an accurate picture of profitability.

Common Mistakes in ROI Calculation

  • Ignoring non-ad costs: Only counting ad spend and ignoring agency fees, software costs, and labor overstates your ROI.
  • Not tracking all revenue: Failing to attribute revenue from brand awareness campaigns or multi-touch journeys understates your ROI.
  • Using the wrong time frame: Some campaigns (especially B2B) have long sales cycles. Measuring ROI too early understates the true return.
  • Confusing ROAS with ROI: A 4:1 ROAS sounds great, but if your product margin is 20%, your actual ROI might be negative. Always factor in margins.
  • Not accounting for lifetime value: A customer acquired through PPC might generate revenue for years. Calculating ROI based only on first purchase understates the true return.
Margin-Aware ROI: If you sell a product with a 30% margin and your ROAS is 3:1, your actual ROI is: ($3 revenue x 30% margin - $1 ad spend) / $1 = -10%. You are losing money despite a 3:1 ROAS. Always calculate ROI with margins in mind.

Conclusion

ROI and ROAS are the ultimate measures of advertising success. ROI tells you whether your campaigns are profitable overall, while ROAS tells you how efficiently each advertising dollar generates revenue. Track both metrics, compare them against industry benchmarks, and use the optimization strategies above to steadily improve your returns. Remember to account for margins, lifetime value, and all costs to get an accurate picture of your true advertising profitability.
