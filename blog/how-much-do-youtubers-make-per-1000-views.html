How Much Do YouTubers Make Per 1,000 Views in 2026?

Real earnings data broken down by niche, format, and revenue source. See exactly what creators earn per 1,000 views.

One of the most common questions from aspiring YouTubers is: "How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?" The answer depends on your niche, content format, audience location, and revenue streams. In this guide, we break down the real numbers for 2026 with data-backed estimates across different scenarios.

Average YouTube Pay Per 1,000 Views

For long-form videos (standard horizontal videos), the average YouTube pay per 1,000 views in 2026 is:

  • Average RPM (what creators earn): $3 - $5 per 1,000 views
  • Average CPM (what advertisers pay): $6 - $12 per 1,000 impressions

The gap between CPM and RPM exists because YouTube takes a 45% cut of ad revenue, and not every view generates an ad impression (due to ad blockers, YouTube Premium viewers, and viewers who skip before ads play).

For YouTube Shorts:

  • Average RPM: $0.01 - $0.06 per 1,000 views

Shorts pay dramatically less per view because of the lower ad load and different revenue sharing model (45% to creators vs. 55% for long-form).

Shorts vs. Long-Form Earnings

The earnings gap between Shorts and long-form is enormous:

Metric Long-Form Shorts Difference
Average RPM $3 - $5 $0.01 - $0.06 50x - 500x
Revenue share 55% to creator 45% to creator 10% less for Shorts
Ad format Pre-roll, mid-roll, display Feed ads between videos Less ad inventory for Shorts
1M views earnings $3,000 - $5,000 $10 - $60 Massive gap

If your goal is to maximize revenue, prioritize long-form content. Use Shorts as a discovery tool to attract new viewers to your channel, then convert them to long-form viewers where the real monetization happens.

Revenue Sources Beyond Ads

Ad revenue is just one part of a YouTuber's income. Successful creators diversify across multiple revenue streams:

  • Ad revenue: The base income from YouTube ads. Typically 30-50% of total earnings for mid-size creators.
  • Channel memberships: Viewers pay $4.99/month for exclusive perks. Creator keeps 70% ($3.49). A channel with 500 members earns $1,745/month from memberships alone.
  • Super Chat: During live streams, viewers pay to highlight their messages. Top creators earn $5,000-$50,000+ per live stream from Super Chat.
  • Super Thanks: One-time tips on individual videos ($2-$50 per tip). Creator keeps 70%.
  • Sponsorships: Brand deals are often the largest revenue source. Typical rates: $20-$50 per 1,000 views for sponsored integrations. A video with 100K views might earn $2,000-$5,000 from a sponsor.
  • Affiliate marketing: Product links in descriptions earn commissions. Finance and tech channels can earn $1,000-$10,000/month from affiliates.
  • Merchandise: Branded products sold through YouTube's merch shelf or external stores. Margins vary but can add 10-20% to total revenue.
  • Course/product sales: Many creators sell digital products (courses, ebooks, templates) to their audience. This can be the most profitable revenue stream.

Takeaway: Ad revenue is often less than half of a successful YouTuber's total income. The creators earning the most are those who diversify into sponsorships, affiliates, memberships, and product sales.

Factors That Affect Your Earnings

Your actual earnings per 1,000 views depend on several factors:

  • Audience geography: US/UK/CA/AU viewers generate 3-10x more revenue than viewers from developing countries
  • Viewer age: Older viewers (25-44) generate higher CPMs because they have more purchasing power
  • Video length: Videos over 8 minutes can include mid-roll ads, increasing revenue per view by 30-50%
  • Season: Q4 (Oct-Dec) CPMs are 50-100% higher than Q1 due to holiday advertising
  • Content topic: Trending topics attract more advertiser competition, temporarily boosting CPMs
  • Audience retention: Higher retention means more ad impressions per view
  • Ad blocker usage: Desktop viewers have higher ad blocker rates, reducing monetized views

Conclusion

On average, YouTubers make $3-$5 per 1,000 views from ad revenue alone for long-form content. However, niche selection can push this from $1.50 (entertainment) to $28+ (finance). Shorts pay dramatically less at $0.01-$0.06 per 1,000 views. The highest-earning creators diversify beyond ads into sponsorships, memberships, affiliates, and product sales.
