What Is YouTube RPM?

YouTube RPM (Revenue Per Mille) is the amount of money a YouTube creator earns per 1,000 video views. Unlike CPM, which only measures advertising costs, RPM captures all revenue sources including ad revenue, Super Chat, Super Stickers, channel memberships, YouTube Premium revenue, and Super Thanks. This makes RPM the most comprehensive metric for understanding your actual YouTube earnings.

RPM = (Total Earnings / Total Views) × 1,000

For example, if you earned $150 from 50,000 total views in a month, your RPM would be ($150 / 50,000) × 1,000 = $3.00. This means you earned $3 for every 1,000 views across all monetization sources.

YouTube RPM vs. YouTube CPM

Many creators confuse RPM and CPM. Here is the key difference:

  • YouTube CPM: What advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions. This only includes ad revenue and does not account for views where no ad was shown.
  • YouTube RPM: What you earn per 1,000 total views (including non-monetized views). RPM includes all revenue sources and is always lower than CPM because not every view generates ad revenue.
Key Insight: YouTube takes 45% of long-form ad revenue, leaving creators with 55%. For Shorts, the split is less favorable: creators receive 45% and YouTube keeps 55%. This means if YouTube reports a $10 CPM for your long-form video, your ad revenue RPM would be approximately $5.50 (before accounting for non-monetized views).

Why YouTube RPM Varies So Much

Audience Geography

Where your viewers are located is the single biggest factor affecting RPM. Viewers from the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, and Western Europe generate significantly higher ad revenue than viewers from developing nations. A channel with 80% US viewers might have an RPM of $8, while a channel with the same content but 80% viewers from South Asia might have an RPM of $1.50.

Viewer Age and Purchasing Power

Advertisers pay more to reach audiences with higher purchasing power. Content that attracts viewers aged 25-54 (who have disposable income) commands higher RPMs than content primarily viewed by teenagers or children.

Video Length

Videos longer than 8 minutes can include mid-roll ads, which significantly increases the number of ads shown per view. A 15-minute video with 2 mid-roll ads might generate an RPM 2-3x higher than a 5-minute video with only pre-roll ads.

Seasonality

YouTube RPM follows seasonal patterns. Q4 (October-December) sees RPM increases of 30-50% as advertisers compete for holiday budgets. January typically sees the lowest RPMs of the year.

How to Increase Your YouTube RPM

  • Create longer videos: Videos over 8 minutes allow mid-roll ads, increasing ad density per view.
  • Target high-value niches: Finance, business, and technology content attracts higher-paying advertisers.
  • Focus on US/UK/CA/AU audiences: Create content that appeals to viewers in high-CPM countries.
  • Diversify revenue streams: Super Chat, memberships, and Super Thanks add to RPM beyond ad revenue.
  • Upload during Q4: Publish your best content in November and December to capitalize on seasonal RPM spikes.
  • Improve audience retention: Higher watch time means more mid-roll ads are seen, increasing revenue per view.
  • Use end screens and cards: Keep viewers on your channel longer to increase total views and session watch time.
