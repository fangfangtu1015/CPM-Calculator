What Is the Difference Between CPM and RPM?

CPM and RPM are related but fundamentally different metrics that serve different perspectives in the advertising ecosystem. Understanding the distinction is critical for publishers trying to understand their actual earnings.

  • CPM (Cost Per Mille): The amount an advertiser pays per 1,000 impressions. This is the starting point - the total cost of the advertising campaign divided by impressions.
  • RPM (Revenue Per Mille): The amount a publisher earns per 1,000 page views or impressions. This is what actually ends up in the publisher's pocket.

The gap between CPM and RPM exists because not every impression generates revenue (fill rate), and the publisher does not keep the full amount the advertiser pays (revenue share). Two key factors bridge this gap.

RPM = CPM × (Fill Rate / 100) × (Revenue Share / 100)

Example: RPM = $10 × 0.85 × 0.68 = $5.78

Understanding Fill Rate

Fill rate is the percentage of ad impressions that are actually served with an ad. If your website has 100,000 page views but only 85,000 of them show an ad (because no advertiser bid on the remaining 15,000), your fill rate is 85%.

Fill rates vary significantly by platform, ad network, traffic quality, and geography. Premium publishers with high-quality traffic may achieve 90-95% fill rates, while smaller sites might see 60-80%. Factors affecting fill rate include:

  • Ad network demand and advertiser competition
  • Geographic location of your traffic
  • Device type (desktop vs. mobile)
  • Ad placement and viewability
  • Seasonal demand fluctuations

Understanding Revenue Share

Revenue share is the percentage of advertising revenue that goes to the publisher. The rest goes to the platform or ad network as their fee. Different platforms have different revenue share models:

How to Maximize Your RPM

  • Improve fill rate: Use multiple ad networks and header bidding to increase competition for your impressions. The more demand sources competing, the higher your fill rate.
  • Negotiate better revenue share: Large publishers can often negotiate custom revenue share terms with ad networks that exceed the standard rates.
  • Optimize ad placements: Higher viewability rates attract more advertisers, increasing both fill rate and CPM.
  • Target premium geographies: Traffic from the US, UK, Canada, and Australia attracts higher-paying advertisers, increasing both CPM and RPM.
  • Improve content quality: High-quality, original content attracts premium advertisers willing to pay higher CPMs.
  • Use video ads: Video ad formats generate significantly higher CPMs than banner ads, directly boosting RPM.
  • Reduce page load time: Faster pages reduce impression loss and improve viewability, both of which increase RPM.

Why RPM Matters More Than CPM for Publishers

While advertisers focus on CPM to manage their costs, publishers should focus on RPM because it represents actual earnings. A platform might advertise a high CPM, but if the fill rate is low or the revenue share is unfavorable, the actual RPM could be much lower than expected. Always evaluate monetization options based on RPM, not just advertised CPM.
