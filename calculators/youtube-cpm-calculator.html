What Is YouTube CPM?

YouTube CPM (Cost Per Mille) represents the amount an advertiser pays for every 1,000 impressions served on their YouTube video ads. It is one of the most important metrics for anyone running advertising campaigns on YouTube, as it directly determines how far your budget stretches and how efficiently you are reaching your target audience.

The formula for calculating CPM is straightforward:

CPM = (Total Ad Spend / Total Impressions) × 1,000

For example, if you spend $500 on a YouTube ad campaign and your video generates 100,000 impressions, your CPM would be ($500 / 100,000) × 1,000 = $5.00. This means you are paying $5 for every 1,000 times your ad is shown.

Factors That Affect YouTube CPM

YouTube CPM is not a fixed number. It fluctuates based on a wide range of factors that advertisers need to understand in order to optimize their campaigns effectively.

Niche and Industry (2026)

The topic of your content has a massive impact on CPM. Advertisers in high-value industries like finance and insurance are willing to pay significantly more per impression because the lifetime value of their customers is high. Here are the average YouTube CPM ranges by niche in 2026:

Geography and Audience Location

Where your viewers are located dramatically affects CPM. Audiences in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada command higher CPMs because consumers in those countries have higher purchasing power and advertisers are willing to pay more. A YouTube channel with primarily US viewers will typically see CPMs 2-5x higher than a channel with viewers in developing nations.

Seasonality

YouTube CPM follows seasonal patterns. The biggest spike occurs during Q4 (October through December) as advertisers compete for holiday shopping budgets. CPMs can increase by 30-50% during November and December compared to the rest of the year. January typically sees the lowest CPMs as advertising budgets reset.

YouTube Shorts CPM

Key Insight: YouTube Shorts CPM averages around $4 (Digital Applied, 2026), which is significantly lower than long-form video CPM. This is because Shorts are shorter, less engaging for advertisers, and the revenue-sharing model differs from long-form content.

YouTube Shorts have become a major content format, but their CPM is notably lower than long-form video. The average Shorts CPM hovers around $4 because the ads are less intrusive and the viewing experience is more fleeting. Creators should factor this into their content strategy when deciding between Shorts and long-form videos.

Tips to Improve Your YouTube CPM

  • Target high-value niches: Finance, business, and technology content consistently commands the highest CPMs.
  • Focus on US/UK/CA/AU audiences: Create content that appeals to viewers in high-CPM countries.
  • Optimize video length: Videos over 8 minutes can include mid-roll ads, increasing total ad revenue per view.
  • Improve audience retention: Higher watch time signals to YouTube that your content is valuable, leading to better ad placements.
  • Upload consistently during Q4: Take advantage of the seasonal CPM spike by publishing your best content in November and December.
  • Use relevant keywords: Proper titles, descriptions, and tags help YouTube match your content with higher-paying advertisers.

YouTube CPM vs. Other Platforms

YouTube's CPM sits in the middle of the social media and video advertising landscape. It is generally higher than Facebook or TikTok CPMs but lower than premium connected TV (CTV) advertising. The key advantage of YouTube is its intent-based targeting through Google's advertising infrastructure, which often delivers better conversion rates despite a moderately higher CPM.
