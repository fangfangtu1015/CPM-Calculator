What Is CPM (Cost Per Mille)?

CPM (Cost Per Mille) is the price advertisers pay for every 1,000 impressions (views) of an advertisement. "Mille" comes from the Latin word for thousand. It is the standard pricing model for brand awareness campaigns across display ads, video ads, social media, and programmatic advertising.

The formula is straightforward:

CPM = (Total Ad Cost ÷ Total Impressions) × 1,000

For example, if you spend $5,000 on a campaign that generates 2,500,000 impressions, your CPM is $2.00. This means you pay $2 for every 1,000 times your ad is shown.

CPM allows media buyers to compare the relative cost efficiency of different advertising channels, publishers, and campaigns regardless of their total scale. A lower CPM means more impressions per dollar spent, though it should always be evaluated alongside click-through rate (CTR) and conversion rate to assess true campaign effectiveness.

How to Calculate CPM: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Determine Your Total Ad Spend

Include all costs associated with the campaign: media buy, creative production, and platform fees. For a pure CPM comparison, focus on the media cost alone.

Step 2: Count Total Impressions

Get the total number of impressions delivered. This data is available in your ad platform's dashboard (Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager, TikTok Ads Manager, etc.).

Step 3: Apply the Formula

Divide your total cost by total impressions, then multiply by 1,000. The result is your CPM — the cost per thousand impressions.

Example Calculation

A brand spends $8,000 on Facebook ads and receives 1,200,000 impressions:

CPM = ($8,000 ÷ 1,200,000) × 1,000 = $6.67

This means the brand pays $6.67 for every 1,000 times their ad appears. To evaluate whether this is competitive, compare it against the average CPM by country and platform benchmarks.

Tips to Lower Your CPM

  1. Broaden your audience: Narrow targeting increases CPM. Expand demographics and interests to reduce cost.
  2. Improve ad relevance: Higher relevance scores lead to lower costs on Meta and Google platforms.
  3. Test multiple creatives: Run 3-5 ad variations simultaneously. Let the algorithm optimize toward the lowest-CPM creative.
  4. Avoid peak competition: Q4 (Oct-Dec) sees the highest CPMs due to holiday advertising. January and February offer the lowest rates.
  5. Use lookalike audiences: On Meta platforms, 1-3% lookalike audiences often deliver lower CPMs than interest-based targeting.
  6. Consider alternative platforms: TikTok and Pinterest often offer 30-50% lower CPMs than Meta for comparable demographics.
